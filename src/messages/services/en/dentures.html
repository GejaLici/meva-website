Dentures: A Guide to Removable Replacement Teeth

Restore your smile and confidence with custom-made full or partial dentures.

What Are Dentures?

Dentures, often referred to as "false teeth," are custom-made, removable appliances that replace missing teeth and surrounding tissues. They are designed to look and function like natural teeth, resting on the gums that cover the jawbones. Modern dentures are more comfortable and natural-looking than ever before, offering a reliable and often more affordable solution for individuals with significant tooth loss.

Types of Dentures

Dentures are categorized based on whether they replace all or just some of the teeth in a dental arch.

1. Complete (Full) Dentures

These are used when all of the teeth in either the upper or lower jaw are missing.

  • Conventional Full Denture: This type is made and placed in the mouth after all remaining teeth have been removed and the gum tissues have had several months to fully heal.
  • Immediate Full Denture: This denture is fabricated in advance and placed immediately after the teeth are removed. This prevents the patient from having to be without teeth during the healing period, but it requires more adjustments and relining as the jawbone heals and reshapes underneath it.

2. Partial Dentures

A partial denture is used when one or more natural teeth remain. It consists of replacement teeth attached to a gum-colored plastic base, which is often connected by a metal framework with clasps that hold the denture securely in place by attaching to the natural teeth.

3. Implant-Supported Dentures (Overdentures)

For a more stable and secure fit, a denture can be designed to "snap" onto dental implants that have been surgically placed in the jawbone. This provides significantly better retention and stability, especially for lower dentures, and helps to preserve the jawbone.

The Process of Getting Dentures: A Multi-Step Journey

Creating a well-fitting, comfortable denture requires precision and several appointments.

  1. Impressions and Measurements: Your dentist will take a series of highly accurate impressions (molds) and measurements of your jaw to ensure the dimensions of your custom denture are correct.
  2. Wax Models and Try-In: A wax model of the denture is created. You will try this model in your mouth several times so your dentist can check the fit, bite, shape, and color of the teeth before the final version is made.
  3. Casting the Final Denture: Once all adjustments to the wax model are perfected, the final denture is fabricated by a dental laboratory.
  4. Final Fitting: The completed denture is placed in your mouth, and your dentist will make any final, minor adjustments needed to ensure it is comfortable, secure, and looks natural.

Caring for Your Dentures and Your Mouth

Proper care is essential for keeping your dentures and your mouth healthy.

  • Daily Cleaning: Dentures must be removed and cleaned daily with a soft-bristle denture brush and a non-abrasive denture cleanser to remove food particles and plaque.
  • Handle with Care: Dentures are delicate and can break if dropped. Always clean them over a folded towel or a sink filled with water.
  • Overnight Soaking: Most dentures need to be kept moist to retain their shape. They should be placed in a denture soaking solution or water overnight. This also gives your gum tissues a chance to rest.
  • Regular Dental Check-ups: It is crucial to continue seeing your dentist regularly to check the fit of your dentures and to have your oral tissues (gums, tongue, cheeks) examined.
  • Relining: Over time, your jawbone will change shape, causing your dentures to become loose. Your dentist will need to "reline" your denture periodically to ensure it continues to fit properly.

Adjusting to Life with Dentures

Getting used to new dentures takes time and patience.

  • Initial Discomfort: It's normal for new dentures to feel bulky or a little loose at first. Minor irritation or sore spots may develop, which your dentist can relieve with simple adjustments.
  • Eating and Speaking: Learning to chew and talk with dentures requires practice. Start with soft foods cut into small pieces and chew slowly on both sides of your mouth. Practice reading aloud to get used to speaking.
  • Increased Saliva Flow: You may notice a temporary increase in saliva when you first start wearing your dentures. This is a normal reaction that will subside as your mouth adjusts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to get used to dentures?

The adjustment period is different for everyone. While some people adapt very quickly, it's common for it to take several weeks or even a couple of months to become fully comfortable with eating and speaking with new dentures.

Will dentures change how I look?

Yes, and typically for the better. By replacing missing teeth, dentures provide crucial support for your cheeks and lips, preventing the "sunken" facial appearance that can occur with tooth loss. They are designed to restore a natural and complete smile.

Should I use denture adhesive?

A properly fitting denture should stay in place without the need for adhesive. However, some people use a small amount for added security and confidence. If you find that your denture is loose and you are relying on adhesive to hold it in, it is a sign that it needs to be professionally adjusted or relined by your dentist.
