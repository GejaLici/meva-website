Cheekbone Reshaping: A Guide to Malarplasty

Achieve enhanced facial harmony by surgically augmenting or reducing the cheekbones.

What is Cheekbone Reshaping?

Cheekbone reshaping, medically known as malarplasty, is a type of facial plastic surgery designed to alter the underlying structure of the cheeks to create a more balanced and harmonious facial profile. The procedure can be categorized into two main types: cheek augmentation, which makes the cheekbones more prominent, and cheek reduction, which makes them less prominent. By carefully modifying the size and shape of the cheekbones, a surgeon can significantly enhance a person's facial contours.

Why Consider Cheekbone Reshaping?

Patients pursue malarplasty to achieve a variety of aesthetic goals related to facial balance. Common reasons include:

  • To add definition and projection to cheekbones that appear flat or are not well-defined.
  • To restore volume to the mid-face area that has been lost due to aging, leading to a sunken appearance.
  • To reduce the width or forward projection of cheekbones that are perceived as overly dominant or wide.
  • To improve overall facial symmetry and create a more V-shaped or heart-shaped facial contour.
  • To bring the cheeks into better proportion with other facial features like the nose and chin.

Are You a Good Candidate for Malarplasty?

A good candidate for cheekbone surgery is an individual who:

  • Is bothered by the size, shape, or lack of definition of their cheekbones.
  • Is in good physical health and free from medical conditions that could impair healing.
  • Has completed their facial bone growth (typically late teens).
  • Is a non-smoker, as smoking can severely compromise healing.
  • Has realistic expectations about the surgical outcome and understands the recovery process.

Surgical Options for Cheekbone Reshaping

The surgical technique used depends entirely on whether you are seeking augmentation or reduction.

1. Cheek Augmentation (Making Cheeks More Prominent)

  • Cheek Implants: This is the most common method for augmentation. Custom-shaped implants, usually made of solid silicone, are placed directly onto the cheekbones to provide permanent volume and projection. Incisions are typically made discreetly inside the mouth or through a lower eyelid incision, leaving no visible facial scars.
  • Fat Grafting (Fat Transfer): For a more natural approach, fat can be harvested from another part of your body (such as the abdomen or thighs), purified, and then carefully injected into the cheeks to add volume. This avoids implants, but results can be less predictable as not all of the fat will survive permanently.

2. Cheek Reduction (Making Cheeks Less Prominent)

  • Bone Shaving/Burring: For patients needing only a subtle reduction, a special surgical instrument can be used to shave or burr down the bone, reducing its prominence without cutting it.
  • Osteotomy: For a more significant reduction, this complex procedure involves surgically cutting the cheekbone (zygoma) and repositioning it inward or backward, then securing it with tiny plates and screws.

The Malarplasty Journey: Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

Cheek reshaping is performed under general anesthesia. The surgeon will make the planned incisions, carefully create a pocket for an implant or expose the bone for reduction, place and secure the implant or reshape the bone, and then close the incisions with sutures.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Recovery is focused on managing swelling and protecting the face.

Week 1
Expect significant swelling, bruising, and discomfort in the mid-face. If incisions were made inside the mouth, a soft or liquid diet will be necessary. You must keep your head elevated at all times, even while sleeping.
Weeks 2-4
Most of the major swelling and bruising will subside. You can often return to a desk job or light activities. It is critical to avoid any activity that could result in an impact to the face.
Weeks 4-6
Most surgeons will clear you to resume normal activities, including light exercise. The initial results of the new contour will become more apparent.
Months 3-6
The final, refined results will be visible as all residual swelling disappears and the tissues settle completely. The new facial contours are now stable.

Understanding the Risks and Considerations

Like any surgery, malarplasty has potential risks which vary by procedure:

  • Asymmetry, where one cheek appears different from the other.
  • For Implants: Risk of the implant shifting from its position, infection, or being palpable (able to be felt) under the skin.
  • For Fat Grafting: Unpredictable fat absorption leading to asymmetry or the need for a touch-up procedure.
  • For Reduction: Potential for nerve damage causing numbness in the cheek or upper lip (usually temporary), or an unsatisfactory new contour.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I have visible scars?

In most cases, no. Cheek augmentation and reduction are typically performed through incisions made inside the upper mouth or through a lower eyelid incision (transconjunctival). Both of these placements result in scars that are completely hidden from view.

Cheek Implants vs. Fat Grafting: Which is better?

Neither is universally "better"; they serve different goals. Implants provide a more dramatic and predictable change in bone structure and projection. Fat grafting provides a softer, more subtle volume enhancement using your own tissue, which can be great for restoring age-related volume loss.

Are cheek implants safe?

Cheek implants are made from solid, medical-grade materials (like silicone) and have a long history of safe use. They are solid and cannot leak or rupture. The primary risks are related to placement, such as shifting or infection, rather than the implant material itself.
