Dental Crowns: A Guide to Restoring Your Teeth

Strengthen and protect your teeth while restoring their natural appearance with a custom-made crown.

What is a Dental Crown?

A dental crown, often referred to as a "tooth cap," is a custom-fabricated, tooth-shaped covering that is placed over a damaged or weakened tooth. Its purpose is to completely encase the visible portion of the tooth at and above the gum line. By doing so, a crown restores the tooth's original shape, size, and strength, while also improving its overall appearance. It is one of the most common and reliable restorative dental procedures.

Why Would I Need a Dental Crown?

Your dentist may recommend a crown for a variety of functional or cosmetic reasons, including:

  • To protect a weak tooth from breaking or to hold together the pieces of a cracked tooth.
  • To restore a tooth that is already broken or has been severely worn down.
  • To cover and support a tooth that has a large filling, leaving little natural tooth structure.
  • To hold a dental bridge securely in place.
  • To cover a misshapen or severely discolored tooth for cosmetic enhancement.
  • To serve as the final restoration on top of a dental implant.
  • To protect and strengthen a tooth after it has had a root canal treatment.

Types of Dental Crown Materials

Crowns can be made from several different materials, and the best choice depends on the location of the tooth, aesthetic desires, and functional needs.

  • All-Ceramic or All-Porcelain: These crowns provide the best natural color match and are an excellent choice for front teeth. They are also ideal for patients with metal allergies.
  • Zirconia: A type of ceramic known for its exceptional strength and durability, making it suitable for both front and back teeth where chewing forces are greatest.
  • Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal (PFM): These crowns have a metal substructure for strength, covered by a layer of tooth-colored porcelain for aesthetics. They are very durable but may show a dark metal line at the gumline over time.
  • Gold and Metal Alloys: Highly durable and resistant to fracture, these crowns are extremely gentle on opposing teeth. They are often used for back molars where aesthetics are less of a concern.

The Dental Crown Procedure: A Two-Visit Process

Getting a traditional dental crown typically requires two appointments.

Visit 1: Tooth Preparation and Impressions

  1. Examination and Anesthesia: Your dentist will examine the tooth and numb the area with local anesthesia.
  2. Tooth Shaping: The tooth is filed down along the chewing surface and sides to create space for the crown to fit over it.
  3. Impression: An impression (or a digital scan) is made of the prepared tooth. This mold is sent to a dental laboratory where your custom crown will be fabricated.
  4. Temporary Crown: A temporary crown is placed over the prepared tooth to protect it while the permanent crown is being made.

Visit 2: Placing the Permanent Crown

  1. Removal of Temporary Crown: Your dentist will remove the temporary crown and clean the tooth.
  2. Checking the Fit: The new permanent crown is placed on the tooth, and your dentist will meticulously check its fit, shape, color, and bite.
  3. Permanent Cementation: Once you and your dentist are satisfied, the crown is permanently cemented into place.

What About Same-Day Crowns (CEREC®)?

Modern technology allows for another option. Same-day crowns, often created with CEREC® (Chairside Economical Restoration of Esthetic Ceramics) technology, use an in-office CAD/CAM system to design, mill, and place a custom ceramic crown in a single appointment. This advanced process eliminates the need for a temporary crown and a second visit, offering a convenient and efficient alternative.

Caring for Your Dental Crown: Longevity and Risks

A dental crown can last for many years with proper care.

Longevity and Potential Problems

On average, a dental crown can last between 5 and 15 years, or even longer. While the crown itself cannot get a cavity, the underlying natural tooth can still decay at the border where the tooth meets the crown. Potential problems include chipping (especially with porcelain crowns), the crown becoming loose, or falling off if the cement fails.

Essential Oral Hygiene

It is critical to maintain good oral hygiene. Brush twice a day and floss daily, paying special attention to cleaning the area around the gumline of the crowned tooth to prevent plaque buildup and decay.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is getting a dental crown painful?

No. The entire procedure is performed under local anesthesia, so the tooth and surrounding area are completely numb. You may experience some temporary sensitivity to hot or cold after the procedure, but this typically subsides quickly.

How long does a dental crown last?

The lifespan of a crown depends on the material it's made from, your bite, and your oral hygiene habits. With proper care, a crown can last for well over a decade. Avoiding habits like chewing ice or grinding your teeth can significantly extend its life.

What's the difference between a crown and a large filling?

A filling is used to "fill in" a portion of a tooth that has been removed due to a cavity. A crown is used when the tooth has more extensive damage and needs to be completely covered for protection. A crown provides significantly more strength and support to a compromised tooth than a large filling does.
