Dental Fillings: A Guide to Treating Cavities

Understand how dentists repair tooth decay to restore your tooth's health and function.

What is a Dental Filling?

A dental filling is one of the most common dental procedures, used to restore a tooth that has been damaged by decay (a cavity) back to its normal shape and function. The process is straightforward: your dentist removes the decayed tooth material, thoroughly cleans the affected area, and then fills the cleaned-out cavity with a special filling material. By closing off spaces where bacteria can enter, a filling helps prevent further decay and strengthens the compromised tooth.

Why Do I Need a Filling?

Your dentist may recommend a filling to treat several common issues:

  • To Treat a Cavity: This is the primary reason for a filling, to remove decay and stop it from spreading deeper into the tooth.
  • To Repair Chipped or Cracked Teeth: Minor fractures in teeth can be repaired with filling material.
  • To Fix Worn-Down Teeth: Teeth that have been worn down from habits like nail-biting or teeth grinding can be built back up.
  • To Close Small Gaps: The same material used for fillings (composite resin) can be used cosmetically to close small gaps between teeth.

Types of Dental Filling Materials

Several materials are available for fillings, each with its own advantages.

  • Composite Resin (Tooth-Colored Fillings): The most popular choice today. Composite fillings are made of a plastic and glass mixture that can be matched to the exact color of your natural teeth, making them virtually invisible. They bond directly to the tooth, which provides good support.
  • Amalgam (Silver Fillings): A mixture of metals, including silver, tin, copper, and mercury. Amalgam is extremely durable, long-lasting, and less expensive than other materials. However, its dark color makes it very noticeable.
  • Ceramic/Porcelain Fillings (Inlays or Onlays): These are custom-made in a dental lab and then bonded to the tooth. They are tooth-colored, highly resistant to staining, and very durable. This option requires two visits.
  • Gold Fillings: Made to order in a lab, gold fillings are known for their exceptional durability and can last for more than 15 years. They are the most expensive option and require multiple visits.

The Dental Filling Procedure: What to Expect

For most direct fillings, the entire procedure is completed in a single, comfortable visit.

  1. Anesthesia: Your dentist will begin by numbing the tooth and the surrounding area with a local anesthetic to ensure you are comfortable throughout the procedure.
  2. Decay Removal: Using a dental drill or other specialized tools, the dentist will carefully and precisely remove the decayed portion of the tooth.
  3. Cleaning and Etching: The space is thoroughly cleaned of any remaining bacteria or debris. For composite fillings, the tooth surface is then etched with a mild acid to help the bonding agent adhere.
  4. Placing the Filling: The chosen filling material is then placed into the prepared cavity. For composite resin, it is applied in layers, with a special high-intensity light used to harden each layer.
  5. Shaping and Polishing: Once the cavity is filled, your dentist will shape the material to restore the tooth's natural anatomy and check your bite to ensure it is comfortable. The filling is then polished to a smooth finish.

Aftercare and Longevity of Fillings

Proper care can help your filling last for many years.

Immediate Aftercare and Longevity

After your appointment, the numbness will wear off in a few hours. It's common to have some temporary sensitivity to hot, cold, or pressure, which should subside within a few days. The lifespan of a filling depends on the material, with composite lasting 5-7 years on average and amalgam or gold lasting 10-15 years or more.

When to Call Your Dentist

Contact your dentist if you experience sharp pain when biting down, if you notice a crack in the filling, or if a piece of the filling breaks off. These issues can often be easily repaired if addressed quickly.

Direct vs. Indirect Fillings

Fillings fall into two categories based on how they are made.

  • Direct Fillings: These are fillings placed "directly" into the prepared cavity in a single visit. This includes composite resin and amalgam fillings.
  • Indirect Fillings: These require two or more visits. They are fabricated outside the mouth in a dental lab (based on an impression of your tooth) and then bonded into place. This category includes inlays, onlays, and crowns made from materials like gold or porcelain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is getting a filling painful?

No. Your dentist will use local anesthesia to completely numb the tooth and the surrounding tissues before starting the procedure. You may feel some pressure from the instruments, but you should not feel any pain.

Are amalgam (silver) fillings safe?

Yes. Leading health organizations worldwide, including the American Dental Association (ADA) and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), have reviewed extensive research and continue to consider dental amalgam a safe, durable, and effective material for fillings.

How do I know if I have a cavity?

Early cavities often have no symptoms at all. As decay progresses, you might notice tooth sensitivity (especially to sweets), a mild toothache, or pain when you bite down. This is why regular dental check-ups and x-rays are so important—they allow your dentist to find and treat cavities when they are small and easy to repair.
