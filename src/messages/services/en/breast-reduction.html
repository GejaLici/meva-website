Breast Reduction: A Guide to Reduction Mammaplasty

Find relief from physical discomfort and achieve a more proportionate figure with breast reduction surgery.

What is Breast Reduction Surgery?

Breast reduction surgery, known medically as reduction mammaplasty, is a procedure that removes excess breast fat, glandular tissue, and skin to achieve a breast size that is more proportionate to your body. The surgery is designed to create breasts that are not only smaller but also lighter, firmer, and less pendulous. During the procedure, the breast is also lifted to a more youthful position on the chest wall.

Why Consider a Breast Reduction?

For many women, breast reduction is a life-changing procedure that addresses both functional and aesthetic concerns.

Alleviating Physical Symptoms

  • Chronic pain in the back, neck, and shoulders caused by the weight of large breasts.
  • Deep, painful grooves in the shoulders from bra straps.
  • Nerve pain or numbness in the arms or hands.
  • Chronic skin irritation, rashes, or infections under the breast fold.
  • Difficulty breathing or limitations on physical activity and exercise.

Improving Aesthetics and Comfort

  • Feeling that your breasts are too large for your body's frame.
  • Difficulty finding clothing and bras that fit properly.
  • Unwanted attention and feelings of self-consciousness about breast size.

Are You a Good Candidate for a Breast Reduction?

An ideal candidate for a breast reduction is someone who:

  • Experiences physical symptoms due to the weight of their breasts.
  • Is bothered by the large size and proportion of their breasts.
  • Is physically healthy and maintains a stable weight.
  • Has completed their breast development.
  • Is a non-smoker, as smoking significantly hinders healing.
  • Has realistic expectations about the outcome, including the presence of permanent scars.

Understanding the Surgical Technique and Incisions

The main trade-off for smaller, more comfortable breasts is scarring. The specific incision pattern used depends on the amount of reduction required.

Vertical (Lollipop) Incision

This technique involves an incision around the areola and another that runs vertically from the bottom of the areola to the breast crease. It is well-suited for moderate breast reductions with less significant sagging.

Inverted T (Anchor) Incision

This is the most common and powerful technique for significant reductions. It includes the "lollipop" incisions plus an additional horizontal incision hidden in the natural crease beneath the breast, allowing for the maximum removal of tissue and skin.

The Breast Reduction Journey: Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

Performed under general anesthesia, the surgeon makes the planned incisions, removes the predetermined amount of breast tissue, fat, and skin, and then sculpts the remaining tissue into a smaller, more lifted breast shape. The nipple and areola are repositioned to a higher, more natural position before the incisions are closed.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Recovery is focused on comfort and allowing your body to heal.

Week 1
Breasts will be swollen, bruised, and sore. Drains may be in place for a few days. A surgical support bra is worn continuously. Pain is managed with prescribed medication.
Weeks 2-4
You can often return to a desk job or light work. Swelling and bruising will continue to improve. Many patients already report feeling relief from their previous back and neck pain.
Weeks 4-6
Most patients are cleared to resume all normal activities, including most forms of exercise. The new, lighter feeling becomes very apparent.
Months 3-6
The final shape and size of your new breasts will settle as all swelling resolves. Scars will begin the long process of maturing and fading.

Understanding the Scars and Risks

Breast reduction results in permanent scars. While they fade over time, they are a necessary trade-off for achieving a smaller, more manageable breast size. Other risks include:

  • Unfavorable scarring.
  • Changes in nipple or breast sensation, which may be temporary or permanent.
  • Asymmetry in breast size or shape.
  • Difficulty or inability to breastfeed in the future.
  • Fat necrosis (the development of firm lumps within the breast tissue).
  • Infection or bleeding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my insurance cover breast reduction?

In many cases, yes. If the surgery is deemed medically necessary to alleviate documented physical symptoms like chronic pain, skin conditions, or nerve issues, insurance providers often provide coverage. This typically requires thorough documentation from your primary care physician or other specialists and a pre-authorization from the insurance company.

How much smaller will my breasts be?

The final size is a key part of your surgical consultation. Your surgeon will discuss your goals, examine your anatomy, and determine a size that is both aesthetically pleasing and proportionate to your body, while ensuring enough tissue is removed to provide lasting physical relief.

Can I breastfeed after a breast reduction?

It can be difficult, and the ability to breastfeed may be reduced or eliminated. The procedure often involves moving the nipple and detaching the milk ducts. If future breastfeeding is a high priority, this must be discussed in-depth with your surgeon, as some surgical techniques may be more preserving than others.
