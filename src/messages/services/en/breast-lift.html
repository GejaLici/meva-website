Breast Lift: A Guide to Mastopexy Surgery

Restore a youthful, perkier breast profile by surgically lifting and reshaping sagging tissues.

What is a Breast Lift?

A breast lift, known medically as a mastopexy, is a surgical procedure designed to address sagging breasts (ptosis) by raising and reshaping them. Unlike a breast augmentation, a lift does not significantly change the size of your breasts. Instead, it works by removing excess skin and tightening the surrounding tissue to restore a firmer, more youthful contour and to reposition the nipple and areola to a higher, more forward-projecting position.

Why Consider a Breast Lift?

Women choose to have a mastopexy to reverse the effects of gravity, aging, and life events. Common reasons include:

  • Correcting breasts that sag, have lost their shape, and appear elongated or pendulous.
  • Addressing nipples and areolas that point downward.
  • Restoring firmness after pregnancy, breastfeeding, or significant weight fluctuations.
  • Tightening stretched skin and reducing the size of enlarged areolas.
  • Improving confidence and feeling more comfortable in various types of clothing and swimwear.

Are You a Good Candidate for a Mastopexy?

An ideal candidate for a breast lift is someone who:

  • Is physically healthy and maintains a stable weight.
  • Is bothered by the feeling that their breasts are sagging or have lost their shape and firmness.
  • Has nipples that fall below the natural breast crease when unsupported.
  • Is a non-smoker, as smoking can severely compromise healing.
  • Has realistic expectations about the outcome, including the presence of permanent scars.
  • It is often recommended to wait until you are finished having children, as future pregnancies can alter the results.

Understanding Your Options: Breast Lift Incision Patterns

The main trade-off for a better breast shape is scarring. The type of incision used depends on the degree of sagging.

Periareolar (Donut) Lift

An incision is made in a circle around the edge of the areola. This technique is best for women with mild sagging.

Vertical (Lollipop) Lift

This involves an incision around the areola and another that runs vertically from the bottom of the areola down to the breast crease. It is suitable for correcting moderate sagging.

Inverted T (Anchor) Lift

This is the most common technique for significant sagging. It includes the "lollipop" incisions plus an additional incision hidden in the natural crease beneath the breast, providing the most powerful lift and reshaping capability.

The Mastopexy Journey: Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

Performed under general anesthesia, the surgeon will make the planned incisions, remove excess skin, lift and reshape the internal breast tissue with sutures, reposition the nipple and areola, and then close the incisions.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Patience during recovery is key to achieving the best possible results.

Week 1
Breasts will be swollen, bruised, and sore. A surgical support bra is worn continuously. Drains may be in place for the first few days. Rest is paramount.
Weeks 2-4
You can often return to a desk job and light daily routines. Swelling and bruising will continue to subside. Avoid any lifting or strenuous activity.
Weeks 4-6
Most surgeons will clear you to resume most normal activities, including light exercise. You will start to see the new shape of your breasts emerge more clearly.
Months 3-6
The final shape and position of your breasts will become apparent. Scars will begin the long process of maturing and fading, which can take up to a year or more.

Understanding the Scars and Risks

A breast lift results in permanent scars. While surgeons place them as strategically as possible, they are an unavoidable part of the procedure. Other potential risks include:

  • Unfavorable scarring (keloid or hypertrophic scars).
  • Changes in nipple or breast sensation, which can be temporary or permanent.
  • Asymmetry in the size, shape, or position of the breasts or nipples.
  • Infection or bleeding.
  • Potential for difficulty with future breastfeeding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a breast lift make my breasts smaller?

A lift is a reshaping procedure, not a sizing procedure. While it doesn't significantly reduce breast volume, the removal of loose skin and tightening of tissue can result in a more compact, perkier breast that may appear slightly smaller or fit better in clothing.

Can I get a breast lift and implants at the same time?

Yes. This is a very common combination procedure known as an augmentation-mastopexy or "lift with implants." It is ideal for women who have both sagging and significant volume loss and wish to address both issues in one surgery.

How long will my breast lift results last?

The results can be very long-lasting. However, no procedure can stop the natural aging process. Future pregnancies, significant weight changes, and gravity will continue to have an effect on your breasts over time. Maintaining a stable weight and healthy lifestyle can help preserve your results for as long as possible.
