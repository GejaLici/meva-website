Dental Bridges: A Guide to Replacing Missing Teeth

Restore your smile's function and appearance with a custom-made dental bridge.

What is a Dental Bridge?

A dental bridge is a fixed (non-removable) dental restoration that replaces one or more missing teeth by literally "bridging" the gap where the teeth used to be. The structure consists of two main parts: the pontic(s), which are the false teeth that fill the empty space, and the abutments, which are the supports for the bridge. The abutments are typically dental crowns placed on the natural teeth on either side of the gap. Once fabricated, the entire bridge is cemented into place as a single piece, restoring both function and aesthetics.

Why Get a Dental Bridge?

Replacing missing teeth is crucial for your overall oral health, not just for looks. A dental bridge can:

  • Restore your ability to chew food properly and speak clearly.
  • Maintain the natural shape of your face and prevent a sunken appearance.
  • Distribute the forces in your bite evenly, reducing stress on remaining teeth.
  • Prevent your remaining natural teeth from drifting or shifting into the empty space.
  • Complete your smile and restore your confidence.

Are You a Good Candidate for a Dental Bridge?

You may be an ideal candidate for a dental bridge if:

  • You are missing one or more teeth in the same area.
  • The natural teeth on either side of the gap are healthy and have strong roots to serve as supports.
  • You have good overall oral and general health.
  • You are committed to meticulous oral hygiene, which is essential for the bridge's success.
  • You prefer a fixed, non-removable solution for tooth replacement.

Types of Dental Bridges

There are several types of bridges, and your dentist will recommend the best one for your situation.

1. Traditional Fixed Bridge

This is the most common type. It involves creating a dental crown for the tooth on each side of the missing tooth space, with a pontic (false tooth) in between. They are strong and reliable.

2. Cantilever Bridge

This type is used when there are adjacent teeth on only one side of the gap. The pontic is anchored by a crown on just one abutment tooth. It's generally used only in areas of low stress, such as the front teeth.

3. Maryland Bonded Bridge

A more conservative option, the Maryland bridge uses a metal or porcelain framework with "wings" that are bonded to the back of the adjacent teeth. This avoids the need to place full crowns on the neighboring teeth.

4. Implant-Supported Bridge

Instead of being supported by natural teeth, this type of bridge is supported by dental implants. This is an excellent option when multiple teeth are missing or when you want to avoid altering healthy adjacent teeth.

The Dental Bridge Procedure: A Two-Visit Process

Getting a dental bridge typically requires two appointments.

Visit 1: Tooth Preparation and Impressions

  1. Anesthesia and Preparation: The area is numbed with local anesthesia. The abutment teeth are then carefully prepared by removing a portion of the enamel to make room for the crowns.
  2. Impressions: An impression (or a digital scan) of your teeth is taken. This serves as a precise model for the dental lab to custom-fabricate your bridge.
  3. Temporary Bridge: Your dentist will fit you with a temporary bridge to wear, which protects the exposed teeth and gums while your permanent bridge is being made.

Visit 2: Fitting the Permanent Bridge

  1. Removal of Temporary Bridge: The temporary bridge is removed, and the underlying teeth are cleaned.
  2. Checking the Fit: The new, permanent bridge is placed and carefully checked for proper fit, comfort, and bite alignment.
  3. Cementation: Once the fit is confirmed to be perfect, the bridge is permanently cemented into place.

Caring for Your Dental Bridge: Longevity and Risks

The success and lifespan of your dental bridge depend heavily on proper care.

Longevity and Potential Risks

With excellent oral hygiene, a dental bridge can last from 5 to 15 years, or even longer. The primary risk is not to the bridge itself, but to the health of the supporting abutment teeth. If decay or gum disease affects these teeth, the foundation of the bridge is compromised.

Crucial Oral Hygiene

You must clean under the pontic (the false tooth) every day to remove plaque and food debris. Since you cannot floss between the connected crowns, you will need to use special tools like a floss threader, superfloss, or a small interproximal brush to access and clean this area effectively.

Frequently Asked Questions

Dental Bridge vs. Dental Implant: What's the difference?

A bridge uses the adjacent natural teeth as anchors to support a false tooth in the gap. A dental implant is a titanium post that is surgically placed into the jawbone to act as an artificial tooth root, with a crown placed on top. Implants do not involve or alter the neighboring teeth but are a more invasive and typically more expensive procedure upfront.

Is getting a dental bridge painful?

No. The procedure is performed under local anesthesia, so you will not feel any pain while your teeth are being prepared. You may experience some mild sensitivity or soreness in the gums for a few days after each appointment, but this is temporary and easily managed.

How much does a dental bridge cost?

The cost of a dental bridge varies widely depending on the type of bridge, the materials used (e.g., zirconia, porcelain-fused-to-metal), the number of teeth being replaced, and your geographic location. It is important to get a detailed cost estimate from your dentist.
