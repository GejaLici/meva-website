Eyebrow Lift: A Guide to Brow and Forehead Rejuvenation

Achieve a more youthful and refreshed appearance by raising sagging eyebrows and smoothing the forehead.

What is an Eyebrow Lift?

An eyebrow lift, also known as a forehead lift or browplasty, is a cosmetic surgery procedure designed to correct a low, sagging brow and rejuvenate the upper third of the face. By raising the underlying soft tissues and skin of the forehead and brow, the surgery can reduce horizontal forehead wrinkles, improve frown lines between the eyebrows, and place the eyebrows in a more alert and youthful position. The result is a brighter, more rested, and pleasant facial expression.

Why Consider an Eyebrow Lift?

Patients seek a brow lift to address signs of aging in the upper face. Common goals include:

  • To raise eyebrows that are positioned low on the face, creating a tired, sad, or angry appearance.
  • To minimize the deep horizontal creases that can develop across the forehead.
  • To improve the vertical "frown lines" that form between the eyebrows (in the glabella region).
  • To improve vision in cases where severely sagging brows are "hooding" the upper eyelids and obstructing sight.
  • To create a more alert and approachable appearance. A brow lift is often performed in conjunction with a blepharoplasty (eyelid surgery) for comprehensive rejuvenation of the eye area.

Are You a Good Candidate for a Brow Lift?

An ideal candidate for an eyebrow lift is someone who:

  • Is physically healthy and maintains a stable weight.
  • Is a non-smoker, as smoking can impair healing.
  • Has a low, heavy brow or deep forehead and frown lines.
  • Understands the surgical process and has realistic expectations for the outcome.

Surgical Techniques for an Eyebrow Lift

Your surgeon will recommend the best technique based on your facial anatomy and aesthetic goals.

Endoscopic Brow Lift

This is the most modern and common technique. The surgeon makes several small, short incisions behind the hairline. A thin tube with a tiny camera on the end (an endoscope) is inserted through one incision, allowing the surgeon to view the underlying muscles and tissues on a screen. Through the other incisions, the surgeon repositions the tissues, lifting the forehead and anchoring them in a higher position. This method results in minimal scarring and a faster recovery.

Temporal or Limited Incision Brow Lift

This technique involves slightly longer incisions than an endoscopic lift, placed discreetly in the temporal area (temples) behind the hairline. It primarily focuses on lifting the outer one-third of the brow, which is often the first area to droop.

Coronal (Classic) Brow Lift

An older technique that involves a single, long incision from ear to ear across the top of the head within the hair. While it allows for a powerful lift, it is less commonly used today due to the more extensive incision, longer recovery, and higher risk of scalp numbness.

The Brow Lift Journey: Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

A brow lift is typically performed as an outpatient procedure using general anesthesia or IV sedation. The surgeon makes the chosen incisions, carefully elevates the forehead skin, repositions the underlying tissues and muscles, and secures the brow in its new, higher position. The incisions are then closed with sutures or clips.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Recovery is focused on managing swelling and allowing the tissues to heal in their new position.

Week 1
Your forehead will be swollen and bruised; bruising may extend down around the eyes and cheeks. You must keep your head elevated at all times. Discomfort is managed with pain medication.
Weeks 2-4
Most of the significant swelling and bruising will have subsided. You can often return to non-strenuous work. Itching or numbness at the incision sites is a normal part of healing.
Weeks 4-6
You can typically resume all normal activities, including most exercise. You will begin to see a clear improvement in your brow position.
Months 2-6
The final, refined results will become fully apparent as all residual swelling disappears and the tissues settle completely.

Understanding the Risks and Considerations

Like any surgery, a brow lift has potential risks that should be discussed with your surgeon.

  • Scarring, though incisions are designed to be well-hidden within the hairline.
  • Changes in skin sensation, such as temporary or permanent numbness or itching on the forehead and scalp.
  • Asymmetry, where one eyebrow's shape or position differs from the other.
  • An unnaturally "surprised" appearance if the brows are over-corrected.
  • In rare cases, injury to the facial nerves that control the muscles of the forehead.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a brow lift and an eyelid lift?

A brow lift raises the position of the eyebrows themselves and smooths the forehead. An eyelid lift (blepharoplasty) removes excess skin and fat directly from the upper or lower eyelids. Often, what a patient perceives as excess upper eyelid skin is actually caused by a sagging brow. A skilled surgeon can determine the true cause and recommend the right procedure.

Are there non-surgical alternatives to a brow lift?

For patients with mild brow sagging, non-surgical options can provide a subtle lift. Injectables like Botox® or Dysport® can relax the muscles that pull the brows down, allowing the muscles that lift the brows to work more effectively, creating a temporary "chemical lift." However, for moderate to severe sagging, only a surgical lift can achieve a significant and long-lasting result.

How long will my results last?

The results of a surgical brow lift are very long-lasting. While the procedure turns back the clock, it does not stop the natural aging process. You will continue to age from your new, elevated brow position. Maintaining a healthy lifestyle and good skincare regimen can help preserve your results for many years.
