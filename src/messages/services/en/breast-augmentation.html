Breast Augmentation: A Guide to Augmentation Mammaplasty

Enhance your confidence by increasing the size and improving the shape of your breasts.

What is Breast Augmentation?

Breast augmentation, known medically as augmentation mammaplasty and colloquially as a "boob job," is a surgical procedure to increase breast size and enhance their shape, symmetry, and fullness. This is achieved through one of two primary methods: the placement of breast implants (either saline or silicone) or through fat transfer, where fat is taken from another part of your body and injected into the breasts.

Why Consider Breast Augmentation?

Women choose breast augmentation for a variety of personal and aesthetic reasons. Common goals include: 👙

  • Increasing the size and projection of breasts that are perceived as naturally small.
  • Restoring breast volume that has been lost due to pregnancy, breastfeeding, or significant weight loss.
  • Improving the symmetry between breasts that are noticeably different in size or shape.
  • Enhancing overall body proportions and achieving a more balanced silhouette.
  • As a component of breast reconstruction following a mastectomy or injury.

Are You a Good Candidate for Breast Augmentation?

An ideal candidate for this procedure is a woman who:

  • Is physically healthy and has maintained a stable weight.
  • Has realistic expectations about what the surgery can achieve.
  • Feels that her breasts are too small or are asymmetrical.
  • Has fully developed breasts.
  • Is a non-smoker, as smoking can significantly impair healing.

Types of Breast Implants

The two main types of implants are saline and silicone. Saline implants are filled with sterile salt water, while silicone implants are filled with a cohesive silicone gel that many feel more closely mimics the feel of natural breast tissue.

Incision and Placement Options

The incision can be made in the crease under the breast (inframammary), around the areola (periareolar), or in the armpit (transaxillary). The implant itself can be placed either over the pectoral muscle (subglandular) or under the muscle (submuscular), each with its own advantages.

The Breast Augmentation Journey: Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

Performed under general anesthesia, the surgeon makes the chosen incision, creates a pocket for the implant, carefully places and positions the implant, and then closes the incisions with sutures.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Recovery is a gradual process that requires patience.

Week 1
You will experience soreness, tightness, and swelling. A surgical bra or compression garment must be worn. Pain medication will help manage discomfort. Rest is essential.
Weeks 2-4
You can often return to a desk job and light activities. Swelling will begin to decrease. Heavy lifting and strenuous exercise are still off-limits.
Weeks 4-6
Most surgeons will clear you to resume all normal activities, including most exercise. The "drop and fluff" process, where implants settle into their final position, will be underway.
Months 3-6
Your final results will become apparent as all residual swelling resolves and the breast tissues fully relax around the implants.

Understanding the Risks and Long-Term Considerations

Breast augmentation is a major surgery with potential risks that must be fully understood. These include:

  • Capsular Contracture: The most common complication, where the scar tissue around the implant tightens.
  • Implant Rupture or Deflation: Both saline and silicone implants can rupture.
  • Changes in nipple or breast sensation, which can be temporary or permanent.
  • Scarring, infection, or bleeding.
  • The need for future revision surgery, as implants are not considered lifetime devices.
  • Breast Implant-Associated Anaplastic Large Cell Lymphoma (BIA-ALCL), a rare cancer of the immune system.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do breast implants last?

Breast implants are not considered lifetime devices. While they can last for many years, it's likely that you will need a revision or replacement surgery at some point in your life, typically within 10 to 20 years.

What is the 'drop and fluff' process?

Immediately after surgery, implants sit high and tight on the chest. The "drop and fluff" is the process over several weeks and months where the pectoral muscle relaxes, allowing the implant to settle down (drop) into a more natural position, and the surrounding tissues expand (fluff) to accommodate it.

Can I still breastfeed with implants?

For many women, yes. The ability to breastfeed can be affected by the type of incision used and the implant placement, but it is often still possible. This is an important topic to discuss with your surgeon during your consultation.
