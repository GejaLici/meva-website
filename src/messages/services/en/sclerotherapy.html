Sclerotherapy: A Guide to Treating Spider and Varicose Veins

Eliminate unsightly veins and achieve clearer, smoother-looking legs with this simple injection therapy.

What is Sclerotherapy?

Sclerotherapy is a minimally invasive medical procedure that has been the gold standard for decades in treating unwanted spider veins and small varicose veins. The treatment involves injecting a specialized solution, called a sclerosant, directly into the targeted vein using a very fine needle. This solution irritates the inner lining of the vein, causing it to swell, collapse, and seal shut. Once closed, the vein can no longer hold blood. Over time, the collapsed vein is naturally reabsorbed by the body, and blood is automatically rerouted through healthier veins, causing the visible vein to disappear.

What Can Sclerotherapy Treat?

Sclerotherapy is highly effective for improving the appearance of superficial veins, primarily on the legs.

  • Spider Veins (Telangiectasias): Small, web-like clusters of red, blue, or purple veins that appear on the surface of the skin.
  • Small Varicose Veins: Smaller, sometimes bulging, blue veins that are not large enough to require more invasive surgical intervention.
  • Associated Symptoms: In some cases, it can also alleviate mild symptoms associated with these veins, such as aching, burning, or swelling.

Are You a Good Candidate for Sclerotherapy?

You may be an ideal candidate for sclerotherapy if:

  • You are bothered by the appearance of spider veins or small varicose veins.
  • You are not pregnant or breastfeeding.
  • You do not have a history of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or other blood clotting disorders.
  • You are able to commit to the aftercare instructions, including wearing compression stockings.
  • You have realistic expectations and understand that multiple sessions may be needed.

The Sclerotherapy Journey: Treatment and Recovery

The Treatment Session

Sclerotherapy is a quick, in-office procedure. While lying comfortably with your legs slightly elevated, your provider will cleanse the skin and then use a very fine needle to perform a series of injections into the targeted veins. You may feel a mild prick and a slight burning or cramping sensation for a minute or two as the solution is injected. A typical session lasts about 30 to 45 minutes.

Recovery and Results Timeline

Proper aftercare, especially compression, is crucial for achieving the best results.

Immediately After Treatment
Your provider will apply cotton balls and compression to the treated area. You will be fitted for and instructed to wear medical-grade compression stockings or bandages. You will be encouraged to walk around immediately to promote circulation.
First Few Weeks
Bruising, redness, and small raised bumps at the injection sites are normal and will fade. The treated veins may temporarily look darker or more prominent before they begin to disappear. You must wear your compression stockings as directed.
Activity
You can resume normal daily activities right away. However, you should avoid strenuous exercise, hot tubs, saunas, and long plane flights for about one week.
Seeing Results
Spider veins typically fade within 3 to 6 weeks. Smaller varicose veins can take 3 to 4 months to fully resolve. For optimal clearance, a series of 2-4 treatments, spaced 4-6 weeks apart, is often necessary.

Understanding the Side Effects and Risks

Sclerotherapy is very safe when performed by an experienced provider. Side effects are typically mild and temporary.

  • Common Side Effects: Bruising, redness, itching, tenderness, and small raised areas at the injection sites.
  • Hyperpigmentation: Brownish staining or discoloration of the skin along the path of the treated vein. This usually fades over several months but can occasionally be long-lasting.
  • Matting: The development of new, very fine networks of spider veins near the treated area, which often resolves on its own or can be treated.
  • Rare Risks: Include skin ulceration at the injection site, an allergic reaction to the sclerosant, or the formation of blood clots in deeper veins.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is sclerotherapy painful?

The procedure is generally considered minimally uncomfortable. It involves small needle pricks, and you may feel a temporary, mild burning or cramping sensation as the sclerosant is injected into the vein. The discomfort is brief and very well-tolerated by most patients.

Are the results of sclerotherapy permanent?

Yes, the veins that are successfully treated are permanently eliminated by the body and will not come back. However, sclerotherapy does not prevent the development of new spider or varicose veins in the future. As veins are often a result of genetics and lifestyle factors, new ones may appear over time, which can also be treated.

Why do I have to wear compression stockings after the treatment?

Wearing compression stockings is a critical and non-negotiable part of the healing process. The compression helps to squeeze the walls of the treated veins together, ensuring that they seal shut effectively. It also significantly reduces bruising, minimizes the risk of side effects like staining, and improves the overall success rate of the treatment.
