Rhinoplasty: A Guide to Nose Reshaping Surgery

Achieve facial harmony and improve breathing with expert rhinoplasty surgery.

What is Rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty, commonly known as a "nose job," is a surgical procedure designed to change the shape, size, or proportions of the nose. It is one of the most popular cosmetic surgeries because of the central role the nose plays in overall facial harmony. A rhinoplasty can be performed for purely aesthetic reasons, to correct functional issues like breathing problems (in which case it is often combined with a septoplasty), or for a combination of both. The goal is to create a natural-looking nose that is in balance with the rest of your facial features.

Why Consider a Rhinoplasty?

Patients seek rhinoplasty to address a variety of concerns.

Aesthetic Reasons:

  • To reduce the size of the nose in relation to other facial features.
  • To remove a prominent bump or "dorsal hump" on the bridge of the nose.
  • To narrow a nasal bridge that appears too wide.
  • To refine the shape, size, or rotation of the nasal tip (e.g., correcting a bulbous or drooping tip).
  • To correct a nose that is crooked or asymmetrical, often from a previous injury.

Functional Reasons:

  • To correct a deviated septum and improve airflow and breathing.
  • To repair the nose after a traumatic injury.

Surgical Techniques: Open vs. Closed Rhinoplasty

The surgeon will choose the best approach based on your anatomy and the complexity of the changes required.

Closed Rhinoplasty

In a closed rhinoplasty, all incisions are made entirely inside the nostrils. This means there are absolutely no visible external scars. This approach is often preferred for more straightforward cases that involve changes to the nasal bridge.

Open Rhinoplasty

An open rhinoplasty involves the same internal incisions as a closed approach, plus a small incision across the columella (the strip of skin between the nostrils). This allows the surgeon to lift the skin, providing direct, unobstructed visibility of the entire nasal framework. This technique is often necessary for more complex procedures, such as significant tip reshaping or revision surgery. The scar on the columella typically heals exceptionally well and becomes nearly invisible.

The Rhinoplasty Journey: From Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

Rhinoplasty is an outpatient procedure performed under general anesthesia that can take several hours. The surgeon makes the incisions, gently lifts the skin, and then sculpts the underlying bone and cartilage. This may involve removing a hump, narrowing the nasal bones, or reshaping the tip cartilages with sutures and grafts. Once the reshaping is complete, the skin is re-draped and the incisions are closed.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Recovery requires patience, as the final results will emerge over a full year.

Week 1
An external cast or splint will be on your nose. You will have significant swelling and bruising, which may extend to the eyes ("black eyes"). Nasal packing may be in place for a day or two. You must rest with your head elevated.
Week 2
The cast, splint, and any external sutures are removed. You will be able to see the initial new shape of your nose, but it will still be very swollen. Most people feel comfortable enough to return to work or school.
Weeks 4-6
The majority of the swelling will have resolved, and you can typically resume all normal activities, including exercise. The bones are stable at this point.
Months 6-12+
The final, refined results will appear during this period. The last 10-20% of the swelling, especially in the nasal tip, will gradually disappear, revealing the final, delicate contours of your new nose.

Understanding the Risks and Considerations

Rhinoplasty is a safe procedure in the hands of a qualified surgeon, but it is a complex surgery with potential risks:

  • An unsatisfactory aesthetic result or nasal asymmetry, possibly requiring a revision surgery.
  • Difficulty breathing through the nose.
  • Numbness in or around your nose.
  • Noticeable scarring (rare with the open approach if it heals well).
  • A hole in the nasal septum (septal perforation).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is rhinoplasty painful?

The surgery itself is performed under general anesthesia, so it is painless. The recovery is more about discomfort than sharp pain. Patients typically describe feeling very congested and stuffy, with pressure and soreness in the nose and a mild headache for the first few days. This is well-managed with prescribed pain medication.

When will I see the final results of my rhinoplasty?

While you will see a significant change as soon as the cast comes off at one week, this is not the final result. It takes a full year, and sometimes up to 18 months, for all the subtle swelling, especially at the nasal tip, to completely resolve and for the skin to "shrink wrap" to the new underlying framework. Patience is key.

What is a "non-surgical nose job"?

A non-surgical nose job uses injectable dermal fillers to temporarily alter the shape of the nose. It is an excellent option for smoothing out a small bump on the bridge or refining the tip. However, it cannot make the nose smaller, and the results are temporary, lasting about a year.
