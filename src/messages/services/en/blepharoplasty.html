Eyelid Surgery: A Guide to Blepharoplasty

Rejuvenate your appearance by correcting droopy eyelids and under-eye bags.

What is Blepharoplasty?

Blepharoplasty, more commonly known as eyelid surgery, is a delicate surgical procedure designed to improve the appearance and/or function of the eyelids. It can be performed on the upper eyelids, the lower eyelids, or both. The surgery involves the precise removal or repositioning of excess tissue, including skin, muscle, and fat, to restore a more youthful, rested, and alert look to the eyes.

Why Consider Eyelid Surgery?

Patients pursue blepharoplasty for both cosmetic and functional reasons. Key motivations include: 👀

  • Functional Improvement: To correct severely sagging upper eyelid skin that droops into the field of vision (a condition called ptosis).
  • Cosmetic Rejuvenation: To remove loose, sagging skin that creates folds and disturbs the natural contour of the upper eyelid.
  • Removing Under-Eye Bags: To eliminate puffiness and bags under the eyes caused by excess fat deposits.
  • Correcting Droopiness: To address drooping lower eyelids that may show the white of the eye below the iris.
  • A More Rested Look: To reduce a tired or aged appearance caused by excess skin and fine wrinkles around the eyelids.

Are You a Good Candidate for Blepharoplasty?

An ideal candidate for eyelid surgery typically has:

  • Puffy upper eyelids due to excess fatty deposits.
  • Loose, sagging skin on the upper eyelids that may interfere with vision.
  • Bags and excess skin on the lower eyelids.
  • Good overall health and no serious eye conditions like glaucoma or severe dry eye.
  • Realistic expectations about the outcome of the surgery.
  • Status as a non-smoker, as smoking can impede the healing process.

Types of Blepharoplasty Procedures

The surgical approach depends on which eyelids are being treated and the specific goals of the patient.

Upper Blepharoplasty

For the upper eyelid, the surgeon makes an incision within the natural eyelid crease. This allows the scar to be well-hidden when the eye is open. Through this incision, the surgeon removes excess skin and fat and may tighten the muscle to create a firmer, more youthful eyelid contour.

Lower Blepharoplasty

For the lower eyelid, the incision can be made in one of two places. A **subciliary incision** is made just below the lower lash line, allowing for the removal of both excess skin and fat. Alternatively, a **transconjunctival incision** is made on the inside of the lower eyelid; this approach is ideal for patients who only need fat removed or repositioned, as it leaves no visible external scar.

The Blepharoplasty Journey: From Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

Eyelid surgery is usually an outpatient procedure performed with local anesthesia and sedation, though general anesthesia may also be used.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Recovery from blepharoplasty is relatively quick, but following instructions is key.

Week 1
Bruising and swelling will be most prominent. Your eyes may be watery or feel dry. Stitches are typically removed within 5-7 days. It's important to rest with your head elevated and use prescribed ointments or eye drops.
Weeks 2-4
Most of the significant swelling and bruising will have subsided, and you can comfortably return to work and social activities. Strenuous activities and wearing contact lenses should still be avoided.
Weeks 4-6
You can typically resume all forms of exercise. The results will become more refined as the last bits of swelling disappear.
Months 3-6+
The final outcome will be visible. Incision lines will continue to fade, often becoming nearly invisible over time.

Understanding the Risks and Results

Blepharoplasty is generally a very safe procedure when performed by a qualified surgeon. However, like all surgeries, it has potential risks:

  • Temporary dry eyes or irritation.
  • Noticeable scarring if incisions do not heal properly.
  • Difficulty closing the eyes completely.
  • Changes in skin sensation.
  • Infection or bleeding.
  • In rare cases, changes to vision.

How to Choose a Surgeon for Your Eyelid Surgery

The delicate nature of eyelid surgery requires a highly skilled surgeon.

  • Board Certification: Choose a surgeon certified by a reputable board of plastic surgery or ophthalmology (an oculoplastic surgeon).

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my scars be visible?

Surgeons are experts at hiding incisions in the natural creases of the eyelids. For an upper blepharoplasty, the scar is in the crease and virtually invisible when the eye is open. For a lower blepharoplasty, the scar is just below the lashes or entirely hidden inside the eyelid.

How long do the results of a blepharoplasty last?

The results are long-lasting. Upper eyelid surgery results can last for many years, while lower eyelid surgery rarely needs to be repeated. While the surgery turns back the clock, the natural aging process will, of course, continue.

Is eyelid surgery painful?

The procedure itself is not painful due to anesthesia. Post-operative discomfort is typically mild and often described as a feeling of tightness or soreness, which is easily managed with over-the-counter pain medication.
