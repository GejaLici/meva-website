Chin Reshaping: A Guide to Genioplasty and Mentoplasty

Enhance your facial profile and create harmony with chin augmentation or reduction surgery.

What is Chin Reshaping Surgery?

Chin reshaping surgery is a procedure designed to improve the contour of the chin, neck, and jawline by altering the underlying bone and soft tissue structure. The goal is to create a more balanced facial profile. The surgery falls into two main categories: chin augmentation to enhance a "weak" or receding chin, and chin reduction to decrease the size of an overly prominent chin. Medical terms for these procedures include mentoplasty (referring to surgery on the chin, often with implants) and genioplasty (referring to surgery that cuts and repositions the chin bone).

Why Consider Chin Reshaping?

A well-defined chin is a key component of a balanced face. Patients seek chin surgery to:

  • Improve the overall balance of their facial profile, especially in relation to the nose.
  • Correct a genetically "weak," receding, or underdeveloped chin (microgenia).
  • Reduce the size of a chin that appears too large, long, or protruding for the face.
  • Create a stronger, more defined jawline.
  • Correct chin asymmetry or deformities resulting from injury.
  • It is often performed in conjunction with rhinoplasty (nose surgery) to achieve comprehensive profile balancing.

Are You a Good Candidate for Chin Surgery?

An ideal candidate for chin reshaping is someone who:

  • Is bothered by the size or shape of their chin and its impact on their facial profile.
  • Has a properly functioning jaw and a normal dental bite (occlusion).
  • Has completed their facial bone growth, typically in the late teenage years.
  • Is in good physical health and is a non-smoker.
  • Has realistic expectations for what the surgery can achieve.

Surgical Options for Chin Reshaping

The surgical technique is tailored to the patient's specific anatomical needs and aesthetic goals.

1. Chin Augmentation (Enhancing the Chin)

  • Chin Implants (Mentoplasty): A specially shaped, solid implant (commonly made of silicone) is placed over the existing chin bone to increase its size and projection. Incisions are discreetly hidden either inside the mouth along the lower lip or in the natural skin crease just under the chin.
  • Sliding Genioplasty: This is a bone surgery where no implant is used. The surgeon makes a cut through the chin bone (an osteotomy), slides the lower portion of the chin forward to the desired position, and secures it with tiny titanium plates and screws. This powerful technique can also correct vertical chin height and asymmetries.

2. Chin Reduction

  • Bone Shaving/Burring: For patients who need a more subtle reduction in chin prominence, a special surgical burr is used to gently shave down the bone.
  • Setback Genioplasty: Using the same osteotomy technique as a sliding genioplasty, the chin bone can be moved backward instead of forward to reduce an overly projecting chin.

The Chin Reshaping Journey: Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

Performed under general anesthesia, the surgeon makes the planned incision, creates a pocket for an implant or exposes the chin bone, performs the necessary augmentation or reduction, and closes the incisions with sutures.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Recovery involves managing swelling and protecting the lower face.

Week 1
You will experience significant swelling, bruising, and a feeling of tightness. A facial compression garment may be required. If incisions are inside the mouth, a liquid or very soft food diet is necessary. Discomfort is managed with pain medication.
Weeks 2-4
Most of the prominent swelling and bruising subsides. Numbness in the lower lip and chin is common and typically improves gradually over weeks to months. A return to non-strenuous work is often possible.
Weeks 4-6
Your surgeon will likely clear you to resume most normal activities, including exercise. You will start to see the new shape of your chin and jawline more clearly.
Months 3-6+
The final, refined result becomes fully visible as all residual swelling disappears and the tissues settle. The new chin profile is now stable.

Understanding the Risks and Considerations

Chin surgery is generally safe when performed by an expert, but all surgery carries risks:

  • Asymmetry of the chin.
  • For Implants: Shifting of the implant, infection, or erosion of the underlying bone over many years.
  • For Genioplasty: Potential for damage to the roots of the lower teeth, permanent numbness in the lower lip or chin due to nerve injury, or issues with bone healing.
  • General risks such as bleeding or an adverse reaction to anesthesia.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I have a visible scar?

If an intraoral incision is used (inside the lower lip), there is no visible external scar. If a submental incision is used, it is placed in the well-hidden natural crease under the chin, and it typically heals into a very faint, inconspicuous line.

Implant vs. Sliding Genioplasty: Which is better for me?

This depends entirely on your specific anatomy and goals. Implants are an excellent and less complex option for patients who need straightforward horizontal projection. A sliding genioplasty is a more versatile and powerful procedure that can correct more complex issues like vertical height and asymmetry, and it uses your own bone, eliminating implant-related risks.

What about non-surgical chin augmentation?

Yes, non-surgical chin augmentation can be performed using injectable dermal fillers (like Juvéderm Volux or Radiesse). This is an excellent option for those who want a temporary improvement, wish to avoid surgery, or want to "test out" a more defined chin before committing to a permanent procedure. Results typically last 1-2 years.
