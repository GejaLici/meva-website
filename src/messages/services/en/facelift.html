Facelift: A Guide to Facial Rejuvenation Surgery (Rhytidectomy)

Turn back the clock by surgically correcting sagging skin, jowls, and neck laxity.

What is a Facelift?

A facelift, known medically as a rhytidectomy, is a surgical procedure designed to improve the most visible signs of aging in the lower two-thirds of the face and neck. A modern facelift is not simply about pulling the skin tight. Instead, it focuses on repositioning and tightening the underlying supportive layer of tissue called the SMAS (Superficial Musculoaponeurotic System). By lifting this deeper foundation, a surgeon can restore a natural, more youthful contour to the face before gently redraping the skin and removing any excess. The result is a refreshed, long-lasting appearance, not a "wind-tunnel" look.

What Problems Does a Facelift Address?

A facelift is specifically designed to target age-related changes in the lower face and neck, including:

  • Sagging in the mid-face and the descent of cheeks.
  • Deep creases that form below the lower eyelids.
  • Prominent folds running from the nose to the corners of the mouth (nasolabial folds).
  • The development of jowls, caused by loss of muscle tone and sagging skin along the jawline.
  • Loose skin and excess fatty deposits under the chin and in the neck, which can create a "double chin" or "turkey neck."
  • Note: A facelift does not address aging around the eyes or on the brow. These concerns require a separate eyelid lift or brow lift, which are often performed concurrently.

Are You a Good Candidate for a Facelift?

The best candidates for a rhytidectomy are individuals who:

  • Are bothered by significant signs of facial aging like skin laxity and jowling.
  • Still have some skin elasticity and a well-defined bone structure.
  • Are in good physical health and do not have medical conditions that would impair healing.
  • Are non-smokers, as smoking severely compromises blood flow and recovery.
  • Have realistic expectations about what surgery can achieve.

Types of Facelift Techniques

Your surgeon will recommend the best technique based on your anatomy and the extent of correction needed.

SMAS Lift

This is the most widely performed type of facelift. The surgeon lifts and tightens the SMAS layer as a separate step from the skin, providing a durable, foundational lift for the cheeks and jowls.

Deep Plane Facelift

This is a more advanced technique where the surgeon works underneath the SMAS layer, lifting the muscle and skin together as a single, composite unit. This can produce very natural and long-lasting results, particularly for addressing mid-face sagging.

Mini-Facelift (Short Scar Facelift)

A less invasive option ideal for patients with mild to moderate jowling and minimal neck laxity. It involves shorter incisions, typically confined to the area around the ear, and has a quicker recovery period.

Neck Lift

Often performed as part of a full facelift, a neck lift specifically targets the "turkey neck" by tightening the vertical platysma muscles and removing excess skin and fat from the neck, creating a sharper jawline and neck angle.

The Facelift Journey: Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

Performed under general anesthesia, the surgeon makes incisions discreetly within the hairline and along the natural contours in front of and behind the ear. The skin is gently lifted, the underlying SMAS tissue is repositioned and tightened, excess skin is removed without tension, and the incisions are closed with fine sutures.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

Recovery requires patience and a commitment to rest.

Week 1
This is the period of most significant bruising and swelling. Your face may be wrapped in bandages, and small drains may be in place for a day or two. Rest with your head elevated is crucial. Discomfort is well-managed with prescribed pain medication.
Weeks 2-3
Most patients feel comfortable enough to go out in public, often with the help of makeup. Swelling and bruising continue to improve daily. A return to non-strenuous work is usually possible.
Weeks 4-6
You can typically resume all normal activities, including exercise. You will notice a dramatic improvement in your facial contours, though some minor swelling and feelings of tightness or numbness may persist.
Months 3-6
The final, refined results will emerge as all residual swelling resolves, tissues soften, and incision lines continue to fade.

Understanding the Risks and Considerations

A facelift is a major surgery with potential risks that must be fully understood.

  • Hematoma: A collection of blood under the skin, which is the most common complication and may require a procedure to drain.
  • Scarring: While incisions are designed to be well-hidden, unfavorable scarring can occur.
  • Nerve Injury: Temporary or, in very rare cases, permanent injury to the nerves that control facial muscles or sensation.
  • Infection, bleeding, or a poor reaction to anesthesia.
  • An unsatisfactory aesthetic result that may require revision surgery.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do facelift results last?

A facelift provides very long-lasting results and can effectively "turn back the clock" by about 10 to 15 years. However, the procedure does not stop the aging process. You will continue to age naturally from your new, more youthful starting point. Maintaining a healthy lifestyle and protecting your skin from the sun can help preserve your results.

What is the 'best' age to get a facelift?

There is no "best" or "right" age. The decision should be based on the visible signs of aging, not a number on a calendar. The best candidates are individuals who show noticeable skin laxity, jowling, and neck sagging, which can occur anytime from the late 40s through the 70s.

Will I look like myself, but younger?

This is precisely the goal of a modern, well-performed facelift. By repositioning the deeper facial tissues (the SMAS) to where they used to be, rather than just pulling the skin tight, a skilled surgeon can restore your natural facial contours. The aim is to make you look rested, refreshed, and like a younger version of yourself, not like a different person.
