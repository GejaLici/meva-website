What is a Brazilian Butt Lift (BBL)?

A Brazilian Butt Lift, or BBL, is a specialized cosmetic surgery that enhances the size and shape of the buttocks without using implants. The procedure involves a two-step process known as **fat grafting**. First, excess fat is removed from other areas of the body—such as the abdomen, hips, lower back, or thighs—using liposuction. Second, that harvested fat is purified and then strategically injected into the buttocks to improve volume, projection, and overall contour.

Why Consider a BBL?

Patients choose a BBL to achieve a variety of aesthetic goals. Key benefits include: 🍑

  • Increasing the fullness, roundness, and projection of the buttocks.
  • Improving overall body proportions, often creating a more defined "hourglass" figure.
  • Restoring volume to flat or sagging buttocks caused by aging, genetics, or weight changes.
  • Achieving a natural look and feel by using your body's own fat cells.
  • Enjoying the dual benefit of slimming down the donor areas where fat was harvested.

Are You a Good Candidate for a BBL?

A good candidate for a BBL procedure typically meets the following criteria:

  • You have sufficient excess fat in other areas of your body to harvest for the transfer.
  • You desire a moderate increase in buttock size and prefer a natural alternative to implants.
  • You are in good overall health and do not have conditions that impair healing.
  • You are a non-smoker, as smoking can compromise fat cell survival and healing.
  • You have realistic expectations and understand that not all of the transferred fat will survive.
  • You are able to commit to the strict recovery process, especially avoiding sitting directly on your buttocks.

The BBL Procedure: A Two-Step Process

A BBL is a careful combination of liposuction and fat injection.

Step 1: Liposuction (Fat Harvesting)

After you are placed under anesthesia, your surgeon will use liposuction to gently remove fat from the predetermined donor sites. Small, discreet incisions are made to insert a cannula (a thin hollow tube) to suction out the fat.

Step 2: Fat Processing and Injection

The harvested fat is placed in a centrifuge to separate the pure, healthy fat cells from other fluids. This purified fat is then loaded into syringes and meticulously injected in small droplets at various depths and locations in the buttocks to create a smooth, round, and natural-looking contour.

The BBL Journey: From Consultation to Recovery

The Surgical Procedure

The entire BBL surgery is typically performed as an outpatient procedure under general anesthesia and can take several hours to complete.

Recovery and Healing Timeline

The BBL recovery is unique and requires strict adherence to post-operative instructions for optimal results and safety.

Weeks 1-2 (The Critical Phase)
You must avoid sitting or lying directly on your buttocks. You will need to sleep on your stomach or side and use a special "BBL pillow" that transfers pressure to your thighs when you must sit. A compression garment is worn on the liposuctioned areas.
Weeks 3-6
Continue to avoid direct pressure on your buttocks. You may be able to return to a desk job with your BBL pillow. Light walking is encouraged, but strenuous activity is forbidden.
Weeks 6-8
Most surgeons will clear you to resume normal sitting and begin reintroducing light exercise. The majority of swelling in the donor areas will have subsided.
Months 3-6+
Your final results will become apparent. Swelling will be gone, and the surviving fat cells will have established a permanent blood supply. Your new shape is now stable, though it can change with future weight fluctuations.

Safety, Risks, and Fat Survival

Safety is the number one priority with a BBL. The most serious risk is a fat embolism, which can occur if fat is incorrectly injected into the large gluteal veins. It is critical to choose a board-certified plastic surgeon with extensive experience in this specific procedure. Other risks include:

  • Infection or poor scarring.
  • Asymmetry or contour irregularities.
  • Fat necrosis (lumps of non-living fat).
  • Unsatisfactory results due to a low fat survival rate (typically 20-40% of the fat does not survive).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much of the transferred fat survives?

Typically, surgeons expect about 60-80% of the transferred fat cells to survive long-term. Because some fat loss is expected, surgeons may slightly overfill the buttocks to compensate and achieve the desired final volume.

Why can't I sit after a BBL?

Sitting or lying on your buttocks puts direct pressure on the newly transferred, delicate fat cells. This pressure can cut off their developing blood supply, causing them to die. Using a BBL pillow and avoiding pressure is essential for maximizing fat survival and your final result.

Is a BBL permanent?

Yes, the results are considered permanent. The fat cells that survive the transfer process are now living tissue in their new location. However, just like other fat cells in your body, they will expand if you gain weight and shrink if you lose weight.
