Wrinkle Fillers: A Guide to Dermal Filler Injections

Restore volume and smooth away lines for a more youthful, refreshed appearance.

What Are Wrinkle Fillers?

Wrinkle fillers, more accurately known as dermal fillers or soft tissue fillers, are gel-like substances that are injected beneath the skin. Their primary purpose is to restore lost volume, smooth out lines and creases, and enhance facial contours. The vast majority of modern fillers are made from Hyaluronic Acid (HA), a naturally occurring sugar molecule in our skin that holds water and helps keep our skin hydrated and plump. By injecting HA fillers, we can replenish this lost volume and effectively "fill in" static wrinkles from below.

What Can Dermal Fillers Treat?

It's important to know that fillers treat static wrinkles—the lines and folds that are visible when your face is at rest, which are caused by a loss of volume and elasticity. This is different from dynamic wrinkles (like crow's feet), which are caused by muscle movement and are best treated with neuromodulators like Botox®. Common areas for fillers include:

  • Softening the nasolabial folds (the "smile lines" from your nose to the corners of your mouth).
  • Filling in marionette lines (the creases that run down from the corners of your mouth).
  • Restoring volume to sunken or flattened cheeks.
  • Augmenting and defining thin lips.
  • Smoothing vertical lip lines (often called "smoker's lines").
  • Correcting hollows under the eyes (tear troughs).

Types of Dermal Fillers

While Hyaluronic Acid is the most common, there are several types of fillers available.

Hyaluronic Acid (HA) Fillers

These are the most popular fillers worldwide. Their effects are temporary (lasting 6-18 months) and, importantly, can be reversed with an enzyme called hyaluronidase. Popular brands include Juvéderm® and Restylane®, which both offer a range of products with different thicknesses for various applications.

Calcium Hydroxylapatite (CaHA)

This filler, known by the brand name Radiesse®, consists of a thicker gel with microspheres that also stimulate your body's natural collagen production. It's excellent for deeper folds and for enhancing bone structure, like the jawline.

Poly-L-lactic Acid (PLLA)

This is a collagen stimulator, known as Sculptra®. It works more gradually by stimulating your own body to produce new collagen over several months. It is used for broad-area volumization, not for filling individual lines.

The Filler Injection Procedure: What to Expect

The Treatment Session

A filler appointment is a quick, in-office procedure.

  1. Consultation: Your injector will perform a facial analysis, discuss your goals, and map out the optimal injection points.
  2. Numbing: A topical anesthetic cream is usually applied for 20-30 minutes before the injections to ensure your comfort. Most HA fillers also contain lidocaine.
  3. The Injection: The injector uses a very fine needle or a blunt-tipped cannula to strategically administer the filler into the targeted areas.

The entire procedure typically takes between 30 and 60 minutes, and results are visible immediately.

Downtime and Recovery

There is minimal to no downtime. The most common side effects are temporary redness, swelling, and bruising at the injection sites, which typically resolve within a week. You can return to most of your normal activities right away.

Frequently Asked Questions

Fillers vs. Botox®: What's the difference?

They treat different types of wrinkles. Botox® is a neuromodulator that works by relaxing the muscles that *cause* dynamic wrinkles (like crow's feet and frown lines that appear with expression). Fillers work by physically "filling in" static wrinkles and restoring lost volume. They are often used together for a comprehensive facial rejuvenation known as a "liquid facelift."

How long do wrinkle fillers last?

The longevity of your results depends on the type of filler used, the area treated, and your body's individual metabolism. Hyaluronic acid fillers, the most common type, typically last anywhere from 6 to 18 months before the body naturally absorbs them.

Is the procedure painful?

Discomfort is usually minimal. A topical numbing cream is applied beforehand, and most modern fillers are pre-mixed with a local anesthetic (lidocaine) to make the injection process very comfortable. You may feel a slight pinch and some pressure during the treatment.
