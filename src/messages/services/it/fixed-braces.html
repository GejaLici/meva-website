Fixed Braces: Your Guide to a Straighter Smile

Understanding how traditional braces work to correct your bite and straighten your teeth.

What Are Fixed Braces?

Fixed braces are the most common type of orthodontic appliance used to straighten teeth and correct bite problems. They are called "fixed" because they are temporarily bonded to the teeth by an orthodontist and cannot be removed by the patient. The system works by applying gentle, continuous pressure to the teeth over time, guiding them into their correct positions. The main components are brackets (small squares bonded to each tooth), an archwire (a metal wire that runs through the brackets and applies gentle pressure), and ligatures (small elastic bands or metal ties that hold the wire to the brackets).

What Orthodontic Problems Do Braces Correct?

Braces are highly effective at treating a wide range of orthodontic issues, known as malocclusions:

  • Crowding: When there isn't enough space for all the teeth.
  • Spacing/Gaps: When there are noticeable gaps between teeth.
  • Overbite: When the upper front teeth stick out too far over the lower teeth.
  • Underbite: When the lower teeth are too far forward or the upper teeth too far back.
  • Crossbite: When the upper teeth do not come down slightly in front of the lower teeth when biting together normally.
  • Open Bite: When a space exists between the biting surfaces of the front or side teeth when the back teeth are together.

Types of Fixed Braces

While metal braces are the most well-known, several options are available to suit different aesthetic preferences and needs.

Traditional Metal Braces

Made of high-grade stainless steel, these are the most common type of braces. Modern metal braces are smaller and more comfortable than ever before. Patients can often customize the look with colored elastic ligatures.

Ceramic Braces

These work in the same way as metal braces, but the brackets are made of a clear or tooth-colored ceramic material, making them much less noticeable and a popular choice among adults and older teenagers.

Lingual Braces

The brackets and wires are placed on the inside (lingual side) of the teeth, making them completely invisible from the outside. They are more difficult to clean and can be more uncomfortable initially.

The Orthodontic Journey: From Consultation to Removal

Orthodontic treatment is a process that occurs over many months or years.

1. Consultation and Planning

Your journey begins with a thorough examination, including X-rays, photos, and impressions or digital scans of your teeth. Your orthodontist will use this information to create a precise, personalized treatment plan.

2. Getting Your Braces On (Bonding)

This is a painless appointment that takes about 1-2 hours. Your teeth are cleaned and conditioned, and the orthodontist carefully bonds the brackets to each tooth. The archwire is then placed and secured with ligatures.

3. Regular Adjustments

You will visit your orthodontist every 4 to 8 weeks. During these appointments, your progress is checked, and your archwires are adjusted or replaced to continue guiding your teeth. It's normal to feel some soreness for a day or two after each adjustment.

4. Retention: The Final, Crucial Phase

Once your teeth are perfectly aligned, your braces are removed. To keep your teeth from shifting back to their original positions, you will be fitted with a retainer. Wearing your retainer exactly as instructed is critical to maintaining your new, beautiful smile for life.

Living with Braces: Oral Hygiene and Diet

Caring for your teeth with braces requires extra effort, but it is essential for a healthy outcome.

Meticulous Oral Hygiene

Brackets and wires create many small spaces where food particles and plaque can get trapped. This increases the risk of cavities and gum disease. You must brush thoroughly after every meal and use special tools like interdental brushes or a water flosser to clean effectively around your braces.

Dietary Adjustments

To avoid damaging your braces, you should avoid certain foods. This includes hard foods (like nuts and hard candies), sticky foods (like caramel and gummy bears), and foods you have to bite into with your front teeth (like whole apples or corn on the cob - cut them up first!).

Frequently Asked questions

Do braces hurt?

Getting your braces placed does not hurt. However, for a few days after they are first put on and after each adjustment appointment, your teeth and gums are likely to feel sore and tender. This temporary discomfort is a sign that the braces are working and can be easily managed with over-the-counter pain relievers.

How long will I have to wear braces?

The total treatment time varies greatly depending on the individual case, including the severity of the problem and patient compliance. On average, most people wear braces for 18 to 24 months, but treatment can range from as little as 6 months to over 3 years.

Braces vs. Clear Aligners: What's the difference?

Fixed braces are bonded to the teeth and are highly effective for treating even the most complex orthodontic issues. Clear aligners (like Invisalign®) are removable, virtually invisible plastic trays. They are an excellent aesthetic option for more moderate crowding or spacing issues but require strict compliance (wearing them 22 hours a day) to be effective. Your orthodontist is the best person to determine which appliance is right for you.
